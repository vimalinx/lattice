# When to use
- The learner already has the relevant foundation, and the conclusion can be reached by reasoning.
- The learner asks “why”, and telling them directly would make them miss the chance to figure it out for themselves.
- In a practice they're stuck, but only one or two steps away from the answer.

# Steps
1. **Set the destination**: first write down for yourself the conclusion they should reach on their own this round (one sentence), and the 2–5 steps they have to climb from what they understand now to that conclusion.
2. **One question at a time**: each question moves them up just one step, asking about something they can answer (predict, give an example, compare, find a counterexample); use `lattice:quiz` to have them answer first.
3. **Adjust to the answers**:
   - Right: confirm which point was right, then ask about the next step.
   - Wrong: don't say “wrong”; ask a question that brings the contradiction into the open (“So what would happen in this example?”).
   - Stuck twice in a row: make the question smaller, or give a concrete situation for them to work through.
4. **Let them say the conclusion**: at the end, ask them to state the whole conclusion themselves; then you add its name and where it applies.
5. Log one judged entry with record: whether they got there on their own, or at which step they needed a hint.

# Don't
- Don't give the answer in disguise with a rhetorical question (“Isn't it X?”).
- Don't fire off three questions in a row; one at a time.
- When the learner says plainly that they want the answer directly, give it, then check with one question whether they understood.
