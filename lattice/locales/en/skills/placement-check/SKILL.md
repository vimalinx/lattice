# How
1. **Look at the evidence first**: what's recorded under "About the learner", the graph, the stage of cards, the answers in the journal. Don't ask again about anything there is already evidence for.
2. **Give one survey** (`lattice:survey`, 4 to 8 questions, all at once):
   - Half ask about background and goals: what they're learning it for, what level they want to reach, related experience (for music, ask whether they play an instrument and whether they can read sheet music; for programming, ask what they've written). Use options; use open answers sparingly.
   - Half are short questions, from easy to hard along the route, each tied to one stop, testing "can they use it" rather than "do they know the name", and every one has an "I don't know" option.
   - In intro, say in one sentence why you're asking: so you can start teaching from the right place. This round ends with the survey.
3. **Place them**: when the survey comes back, state your judgment in a sentence or two (what they already know / what's shaky / which stop they haven't reached) and which questions it's based on; use route to mark the stops they already know as done and the starting point as now, record the shaky places with insight, and record background that holds across topics with remember.
4. Then start teaching from the starting point right away.

# Don't
- Making it an exam; it should take two minutes to fill in.
- Deciding they don't know a whole stop because of one wrong answer: for a stop you're unsure of, confirm it first with one `lattice:quiz` when you start teaching it.
- Pressing on when the learner has skipped the survey.
