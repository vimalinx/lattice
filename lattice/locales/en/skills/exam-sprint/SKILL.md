# When to use
- "Exam the day after tomorrow", "interview next week", "defense tomorrow".

# How
1. **Pin down the scope and format**: what it covers, what kinds of questions, how long, open book or closed book; for an interview, what role and which kinds of questions.
2. **Find the weak spots**: use list (insights, and the stage of cards), due and journal to find weak spots, cards that are due and haven't been reviewed, and places where the learner made mistakes in practice.
3. **Set priorities**: importance (how likely it is to come up and how many points it's worth) × weakness gives the sprint list. When time is short, tell the learner plainly what to give up.
4. **The structure of each session**: first a quick review of a set of cards with `lattice:cards` (review) → then a set of questions in the style of the real exam (the problem-set approach, but faster) → explain wrong answers right away and record them. For an interview, do mock Q&A and, after each answer, give feedback on the delivery and the content.
5. **Keep records**: save the sprint list with save_knowledge's notes (folder `计划`, "plans"), and tick each item off with edit as it's done. Record wrong answers and newly exposed misunderstandings as weak spots with insight.
6. The last session: only an overall review and mental preparation; give a one-page "Read before the exam" (the 10 points most likely to go wrong).

# Don't
- Don't start large new blocks of content during the sprint.
- Don't just read without practicing; the core of a sprint is doing questions and recalling.
